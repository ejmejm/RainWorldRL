# Model-free exploration bonuses, their robustness, and the continuing (no-reset) setting

Literature review for a pixel-observation, continuing (death = respawn, no reset), singleton open-world environment (Rain World) with a 9-key MultiBinary action space. Baseline planned: PPO + RND.

Date of review: 2026-10-04. Everything below was checked against the arXiv abstract page, the paper PDF/HTML, or the GitHub repository on that date unless marked **[not verified]**. Numbers read off figures (rather than tables) are marked *approx.*

Constraints applied throughout: no game-specific tricks (no hand-tuned action repeat, no hand-designed cell representations), no state restore (Go-Explore family excluded), must handle large pixel observation spaces, no LLM/VLM-derived rewards (Motif and similar excluded).

---

## 0. Executive summary

* **Best-evidenced bonus for a fixed ("singleton") pixel world without resets: RND**, specifically the Burda et al. (2018) recipe with a *non-episodic* intrinsic value head, a separate episodic extrinsic head, running-std intrinsic reward normalisation, whitened+clipped observations for the predictor/target only, and a predictor update proportion that fixes the effective predictor batch size. The theoretical argument in Henaff et al. (2023) (global bonuses win when structure is shared across episodes; a singleton MDP is the extreme case) and their Montezuma experiment (RND global bonus >> E3B episodic bonus) both point the same way. RND is also the only method in this stream that was *designed* to be non-episodic.
* **Episodic bonuses (E3B, NovelD's episodic gate, RIDE's episodic counts, NGU's episodic memory) all assume an episode boundary to reset their memory.** In a continuing env they must be given an artificial "pseudo-episode" (fixed window, or reset on death/respawn). The E3B paper itself shows that the lifetime (non-episodic) variant of E3B is much worse than episodic E3B on MiniHack, and Henaff 2023 shows the episodic bonus alone fails on Montezuma. So in a singleton world they are *complements* to a global bonus, not replacements.
* **Robustness evidence is sobering.** Taïga et al. (ICLR 2020): on Atari with Rainbow, RND/ICM/pseudo-counts match but never beat epsilon-greedy outside Montezuma. Craftax (ICML 2024): RND/ICM/E3B did not improve over PPO at 1B steps and E3B hurt. NLE (NeurIPS 2020): RND-only agents made no progress; RND helped some tasks and hurt others. RLeXplore (TMLR 2025): nearly every bonus fails without reward normalisation; RND gets zero reward on Mario without observation RMS normalisation. EIPO (NeurIPS 2022): fixed intrinsic coefficients hurt when extrinsic reward is dense. The consistent lesson is that *implementation details and the intrinsic/extrinsic balance matter more than the choice of bonus*.
* **No standard benchmark exists for continuing, pixel-based, no-reset exploration.** The closest things are: Jelly Bean World (ICLR 2020; non-episodic grid world with small image + scent observations, used by Steinparz et al. 2022 for lifelong intrinsic motivation), EARL (NeurIPS 2021 D&B; reset-free but state-based robotics, with demonstrations), Forager (2026; continuing grid world for continual RL), and the "continuing Atari/MuJoCo" testbeds of Wan et al. (2025), which study reward centering and discounting but not intrinsic motivation. Rain World as a continuing pixel exploration testbed would be genuinely new.
* **Implement first:** (1) PPO + RND exactly per Burda 2018 / CleanRL `ppo_rnd_envpool.py`, treating death as a non-terminal for the intrinsic head and as a terminal for the extrinsic head; (2) E3B as a pseudo-episodic bonus multiplied into RND (Henaff 2023 combined bonus), with the covariance reset on respawn or on a fixed window; (3) Random Latent Exploration (RLE) as a cheap, bonus-free alternative that was the only method with statistically reliable gains over PPO across all 57 Atari games in its paper, with the latent resampled on a fixed-step schedule rather than at episode ends.

## Part 1. Model-free knowledge / novelty bonuses

Template: (1) name/authors/year/venue/arXiv; (2) algorithm; (3) episodic assumptions and continuing adaptation; (4) observation type validated; (5) robustness evidence; (6) code; (7) fit.

### 1.1 RND - Random Network Distillation

1. **Exploration by Random Network Distillation.** Burda, Edwards, Storkey, Klimov. 2018, ICLR 2019. arXiv:1810.12894 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894).
2. **Algorithm.** A fixed random target network f(o) and a trained predictor f_hat(o); bonus i_t = ||f_hat(o_{t+1}) - f(o_{t+1})||^2. Predictor trained by MSE on the agent's observations. Intrinsic reward normalised by dividing by a running estimate of the std of the *discounted intrinsic return* (the `RewardForwardFilter`). Observations for target/predictor are whitened per pixel with running mean/std and clipped to [-5,5]; normalisation statistics are initialised by stepping a random agent for M steps (CleanRL: 50 iterations x 128 steps). Policy network sees the usual stacked uint8 frames, *not* the whitened ones. Two value heads V_E, V_I; returns computed separately with gamma_E = 0.999 and gamma_I = 0.99; advantages combined as 2*A_E + 1*A_I. The intrinsic return is **non-episodic** (done flags ignored when bootstrapping V_I), the extrinsic return is episodic. Predictor is trained on a random 25% of each batch at 128 envs (keep probability chosen to match the predictor batch size of a 32-env run; 0.125 at 256 envs, 0.03125 at 1024 envs). Sticky actions p = 0.25, no terminal-on-life-loss, no intrinsic reward clipping. Default PPO: lr 1e-4, rollout 128, 4 epochs, 4 minibatches, entropy 0.001, lambda 0.95, clip 0.1, 128 envs, 30K rollouts (1.97B frames).
3. **Episodic assumptions.** None for the bonus itself; the paper explicitly argues (Sec. 2.3) that intrinsic return should be non-episodic because "the agent's intrinsic return should be related to all the novel states that it could find in the future, regardless of whether they all occur in one episode or are spread over several", and shows (Sec. 3.1/3.2, Fig. 3/4) that the non-episodic intrinsic stream finds more rooms. The extrinsic head is episodic to prevent the "find a reward, die, repeat" exploit. In a continuing env with death = respawn, this maps directly: the intrinsic head is already continuing; the extrinsic head should still treat death as a terminal (or you must design the extrinsic signal so that dying is not profitable). The CleanRL implementation keeps `int_nextnonterminal = 1.0` always, so nothing changes for the intrinsic branch.
4. **Observations validated.** 84x84 grayscale Atari pixels (frame stack 4 for policy, single frame for RND). Also symbolic NetHack (NLE paper), symbolic Craftax, Habitat RGB (Henaff 2023), many others.
5. **Robustness evidence.** Positive: Montezuma mean 8,152 (RNN policy, 1.97B frames), best run 17,500; Table 1 also Gravitar 3,906, PrivateEye 8,666, Venture 1,859; Henaff 2023 Fig. 5b: RND global bonus works on Montezuma where E3B episodic bonus fails; NLE: RND gives +13.6/+6.5/+16.3 pp on `staircase` for monk/tourist/valkyrie but -13.0 pp for wizard, consistent gains on `pet`, `gold`, `score`, consistently *worse* on `eat`; RND-only made "no significant progress" in NetHack. Negative: Taïga et al. 2020 - with Rainbow at 200M frames RND ~5,000 on Montezuma (same as CTS/PixelCNN), but on easy-exploration games "RND and ICM are able to consistently match epsilon-greedy exploration but never beat it", and nothing improved with 1B frames; Craftax-1B - RND below PPO (approx. 35-40% vs 40-45% of max reward); RLeXplore - RND gets zero reward on SuperMarioBros without RMS observation normalisation; Burda 2018 Sec. 3.6 notes sticky-action non-determinism creates a mild noisy-TV effect near room boundaries. Known failure mode: dependence on observation normalisation and on the rate at which the predictor learns (update proportion).
6. **Code.** Official: https://github.com/openai/random-network-distillation (TensorFlow 1, MPI; archived read-only 2026-04-08, "no updates expected"). Best maintained reimplementation: CleanRL `ppo_rnd_envpool.py` (PyTorch + EnvPool; docs https://docs.cleanrl.dev/rl-algorithms/ppo-rnd/ report 7,100 on Montezuma for a single seed vs 8,152 in the paper; defaults: total_timesteps 2e9, lr 1e-4, 128 envs x 128 steps, gamma 0.999, int_gamma 0.99, int_coef 1.0, ext_coef 2.0, update_proportion 0.25, num_iterations_obs_norm_init 50, 4 minibatches, 4 epochs, ent_coef 0.001, clip_coef 0.1, max_grad_norm 0.5, anneal_lr, norm_adv, gae_lambda 0.95). JAX: Craftax_Baselines `ppo_rnd.py` (https://github.com/MichaelTMatthews/Craftax_Baselines; Flax/Optax, symbolic obs, separate V_e/V_i heads, `rnd_is_episodic=False` default, but **no observation or reward normalisation and a single gamma** - a simplified RND, not a faithful port). Also RLeXplore (PyTorch, plug-in modules), deep_rl_zoo `ppo_rnd` (PyTorch, Pong/Breakout only). I did not find a purejaxrl/rejax RND.
7. **Fit: good.** This is the only bonus here designed for non-episodic intrinsic returns, validated on pixels at scale, with a high-quality single-file reference implementation.

### 1.2 ICM - Intrinsic Curiosity Module

1. **Curiosity-driven Exploration by Self-supervised Prediction.** Pathak, Agrawal, Efros, Darrell. ICML 2017. arXiv:1705.05363.
2. **Algorithm.** Encoder phi trained by an inverse-dynamics head (predict a_t from phi(s_t), phi(s_{t+1})); forward model predicts phi(s_{t+1}) from (phi(s_t), a_t); bonus = forward prediction error in feature space. Base A3C in the paper. Combined as r = r_e + eta * r_i (single value head in the original).
3. **Episodic assumptions.** None in the bonus. Original experiments were episodic (VizDoom, Mario) with a single value head; in a continuing env use the RND-style two-head, non-episodic intrinsic return. Note the "Large-Scale Study" (below) deliberately treated death as non-terminal for the curiosity agent.
4. **Observations.** Pixels (VizDoom, Super Mario Bros); Atari in Burda et al. 2018 (large-scale study); URLB pixels (DMC).
5. **Robustness evidence.** URLB: ICM was the *leading* method on pixel-based URLB (Laskin et al. 2021), though "none of the implemented unsupervised RL algorithms solve the benchmark". Taïga 2020: ICM "unable to surpass the baselines by a large margin" on Montezuma and never beat epsilon-greedy on easy games. Craftax-1B: ICM below PPO (approx.). Large-Scale Study: inverse-dynamics features beat random features in 55% of Atari games, but random features are "a surprisingly strong baseline". Steinparz et al. 2022 used ICM for lifelong non-stationary exploration in Jelly Bean World (see Part 3). Known failure: noisy-TV / stochastic transitions (demonstrated in the large-scale study).
6. **Code.** Official: https://github.com/pathak22/noreward-rl (TensorFlow, A3C). Large-scale-study code: https://github.com/openai/large-scale-curiosity **[existence inferred from the project page https://pathak22.github.io/large-scale-curiosity/, not fetched]**. Reimplementations: RLeXplore (PyTorch), facebookresearch/impact-driven-exploration (torchbeast; ICM baseline), Craftax_Baselines `ppo_icm.py` (JAX).
7. **Fit: good / needs the RND-style two-head wrapper.** Weaker Montezuma evidence than RND but best pixel result on URLB.

### 1.3 Ensemble disagreement

1. **Self-Supervised Exploration via Disagreement.** Pathak, Gandhi, Gupta. ICML 2019. arXiv:1906.04161.
2. **Algorithm.** Ensemble of K forward-dynamics models in a (random or learned) feature space; bonus = variance of ensemble predictions. Because the reward is differentiable w.r.t. actions, the policy can also be optimised by direct backprop (used for the robot experiments); for Atari it is used as a reward with PPO as in the large-scale-curiosity code. Robust to aleatoric noise (noisy TV): the ensemble agrees on the *mean* of a stochastic transition, so variance collapses even when prediction error does not.
3. **Episodic assumptions.** None; same two-head treatment as RND recommended.
4. **Observations.** Pixels (stochastic Atari, Unity 3D maze), MuJoCo states, real robot images.
5. **Robustness evidence.** URLB: implemented as "Disagreement", competitive on state-based DMC, not leading on pixels. RLeXplore includes it; no systematic pixel-Atari sweep found. Main advantage is the noisy-TV robustness, which matters in Rain World (rain, random creatures) more than in deterministic Atari.
6. **Code.** Official: https://github.com/pathak22/exploration-by-disagreement (TensorFlow, 2 commits, built on the large-scale-curiosity code, inactive). RLeXplore has a PyTorch module.
7. **Fit: good (adaptation trivial), evidence thin.** Worth considering as a drop-in replacement for the RND predictor if stochasticity turns out to attract RND.

### 1.4 NGU and Agent57

1. **Never Give Up: Learning Directed Exploration Strategies.** Badia, Sprechmann, Vitvitskyi, Guo, Piot, Kapturowski, Tieleman, Arjovsky, Pritzel, Bolt, Blundell. ICLR 2020. arXiv:2002.06038. **Agent57: Outperforming the Atari Human Benchmark.** Badia, Piot, Kapturowski, Sprechmann, Vitvitskyi, Guo, Blundell. ICML 2020. arXiv:2003.13350.
2. **Algorithm.** Episodic novelty: an episodic memory M of inverse-dynamics embeddings f(o_t) (controllable features) for the current episode; r_episodic = 1 / sqrt(sum over k-NN kernel similarities + c). Lifelong novelty: an RND error alpha_t normalised to a running mean/std; combined r_i = r_episodic * min(max(alpha_t, 1), L=5). A UVFA learns a family of N policies indexed by (beta_j, gamma_j) (beta = intrinsic weight, gamma up to 0.997); off-policy R2D2 (recurrent, Retrace, distributed, 256 actors). Agent57 adds (a) separate Q_e and Q_i networks, Q = Q_e + beta_j Q_i, each with its own Retrace loss "to adapt to the scale and variance" of its reward; (b) a sliding-window UCB bandit per actor choosing j from N = 32 arms at each episode start; (c) gamma up to 0.9999. Trained on ~78B frames across all 57 games; episodic memory capacity 30,000.
3. **Episodic assumptions. Strong.** The episodic memory is wiped at every episode start; the bandit selects an arm per episode and is rewarded with the undiscounted *episode* return. Continuing adaptation: define pseudo-episodes (reset the memory on respawn or every T steps, or use a sliding-window / FIFO memory of the last T embeddings), and reward the bandit with the extrinsic return over the pseudo-episode. There is no published non-episodic NGU that I could find.
4. **Observations.** Atari pixels.
5. **Robustness evidence.** NGU: first non-zero Pitfall (mean 8,400 over all policies), Atari-57 median HNS 1344%. Agent57: above human on all 57 games. Both at enormous sample budgets (tens of billions of frames). Andres et al. 2022 found that training a family of agents with different intrinsic coefficients (their "ngu" strategy) was *worse* than parameter-decay and adaptive-decay schedules on MiniGrid, except for RND. The episodic-kNN bonus is the inspiration for the episodic components of RIDE/NovelD/E3B.
6. **Code.** No official DeepMind release (confirmed by searching; only community ports exist): https://github.com/michaelnny/deep_rl_zoo (PyTorch, NGU + Agent57 + R2D2, tested only on Pong/Breakout, "can't guarantee it's bug free"); https://github.com/YHL04/agent57 (PyTorch, unofficial); https://github.com/Coac/never-give-up (partial, episodic module only). RLeXplore has an "NGU" intrinsic-reward module (the bonus only, not the agent).
7. **Fit: excluded as a full agent (needs adaptation, no reference code, huge compute); the *bonus* (episodic kNN x RND modulator) is worth borrowing** - it is essentially the multiplicative combination Henaff 2023 later found most robust.

### 1.5 RIDE - Rewarding Impact-Driven Exploration

1. **RIDE: Rewarding Impact-Driven Exploration for Procedurally-Generated Environments.** Raileanu, Rocktäschel. ICLR 2020. arXiv:2002.12292.
2. **Algorithm.** Embedding phi trained with ICM-style inverse+forward dynamics; bonus = ||phi(s_{t+1}) - phi(s_t)||_2 / sqrt(N_ep(s_{t+1})), where N_ep is the *episodic* visitation count of the exact state. IMPALA (torchbeast) base; r = r_e + eta r_i.
3. **Episodic assumptions. Strong.** The episodic count term is essential (E3B paper: removing episodic counts from RIDE/NovelD/AGAC "caused complete training failure" on MiniGrid). Exact-state counts also do not exist for pixels; one needs hashing or a learned embedding (hand-designed cells are excluded by the researcher's constraints). Continuing adaptation: pseudo-episodic counts in a hashed/learned space, or drop the count term and use E3B's elliptical bonus instead (which is exactly what E3B did).
4. **Observations.** MiniGrid symbolic/partial-grid; VizDoom pixels (without the count term, matched by E3B on VizDoom).
5. **Robustness evidence.** RLeXplore reproduced and exceeded the Mario result (100% completion vs 23% in the paper) and found E3B+RIDE the best mixed bonus; Andres 2022: RIDE with episodic counts solved all their MiniGrid tasks while RND could not solve MN7S8/O2Dlh under any weighting. No evidence on singleton pixel worlds.
6. **Code.** Official: https://github.com/facebookresearch/impact-driven-exploration (PyTorch/torchbeast, CC-BY-NC, archived read-only 2025-07-17; includes RND/ICM/count baselines). RLeXplore module.
7. **Fit: needs adaptation; superseded by E3B for pixel settings.**

### 1.6 NovelD / BeBold

1. **NovelD: A Simple yet Effective Exploration Criterion.** Zhang, Xu, Wang, Wu, Keutzer, Gonzalez, Tian. NeurIPS 2021. (Earlier version "BeBold: Exploration Beyond the Boundary of Explored Regions", arXiv:2012.08621.)
2. **Algorithm.** Global novelty from RND; bonus = max(RND(s_{t+1}) - alpha RND(s_t), 0) * 1[N_ep(s_{t+1}) = 1], i.e. reward crossing *from* familiar *to* novel states, only on first visit within the episode. Claims to weight novel frontier regions roughly equally and to mitigate RND's tendency to lock onto one novel area; argues this helps with short-sightedness and detachment. IMPALA base.
3. **Episodic assumptions. Strong** (first-visit episodic indicator, exact-state counts). Without the indicator the method degrades badly (E3B paper ablation). Continuing adaptation as for RIDE.
4. **Observations.** MiniGrid, NetHack symbolic; Atari results claimed in the paper **[not independently verified]**.
5. **Robustness evidence.** Solved all 12 hardest MiniGrid tasks at 120M steps; SOTA on several NetHack tasks. Henaff 2023: E3B x NovelD-global is a strong combined bonus on MiniHack.
6. **Code.** Official: https://github.com/tianjunz/NovelD (PyTorch, built on the RIDE torchbeast code, MiniGrid commands only, CC-BY-NC).
7. **Fit: needs adaptation.** The "difference of RND" idea is usable as-is in a continuing env (it is a global signal); the episodic gate is the part that needs a pseudo-episode.

### 1.7 E3B - Elliptical Episodic Bonuses

1. **Exploration via Elliptical Episodic Bonuses.** Henaff, Raileanu, Jiang, Rocktäschel. NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2210.05805.
2. **Algorithm.** Inverse-dynamics embedding phi(s) (dimension d). Within an episode maintain C_t = sum_k phi(s_k) phi(s_k)^T + lambda I; bonus b(s_t) = phi(s_t)^T C_{t-1}^{-1} phi(s_t) (a continuous generalisation of 1/N_ep). C^{-1} updated with Sherman-Morrison rank-1 updates (O(d^2) per step). IMPALA/torchbeast (MiniHack, VizDoom), DD-PPO (Habitat); r = r_e + beta b. Hyperparameters: ridge lambda (Craftax used 0.1), intrinsic coefficient.
3. **Episodic assumptions. By design.** C is reset to lambda I at each episode start. The paper's ablation "E3B (non-episodic)" - covariance accumulated over the whole lifetime - "performs significantly worse" on MiniHack. In a singleton world the lifetime variant converges to a near-zero bonus everywhere; the episodic version keeps rewarding within-window diversity. Continuing adaptation options: (a) reset C on respawn (death as a pseudo-episode boundary); (b) fixed-length window; (c) exponential forgetting C_t = rho C_{t-1} + phi phi^T (an obvious variant, but I found **no published evaluation** of a decayed/forgetting elliptical bonus). Henaff 2023 Sec. 3.2 (Corridors) shows a case where an episodic bonus *alone* fails in a singleton MDP, so pair it with a global bonus.
4. **Observations.** MiniHack symbolic; VizDoom pixels (matched RIDE/ICM); Habitat HM3D RGB (1000 scenes, reward-free coverage, 225M steps) where E3B beat RND/ICM/NovelD.
5. **Robustness evidence.** New SOTA on 16 MiniHack tasks over IMPALA, RND, RIDE, ICM, NovelD. Henaff 2023: E3B alone fails on Montezuma (singleton), E3B x RND nearly matches RND there and is "more robust across scenarios". Craftax-1B: E3B "significantly reduced the reward" relative to PPO, but was the best at the hard EAT_PLANT achievement. RLeXplore: E3B among the best single bonuses on Mario, E3B+RIDE best combination. 2025-26 follow-ups report beating E3B on MiniHack (ETD, ICLR 2025, temporal-distance episodic novelty; CAE+ 2025/26) but these are contextual-MDP results.
6. **Code.** Official: https://github.com/facebookresearch/e3b (PyTorch; torchbeast IMPALA for MiniHack/VizDoom, Sample Factory APPO variant ~10x faster on MiniHack, Habitat DD-PPO; includes the ICML 2023 combined-bonus experiments; CC-BY-NC with Apache/MIT parts; **archived read-only 2024-10-01**). RLeXplore module (PyTorch). Craftax_Baselines `ppo_e3b.py` (JAX).
7. **Fit: needs adaptation (pseudo-episode), recommended as the episodic half of a combined bonus.**

### 1.8 RLE - Random Latent Exploration

1. **Random Latent Exploration for Deep Reinforcement Learning.** Mahankali, Hong, Sekhari, Rakhlin, Agrawal. ICML 2024. arXiv:2407.13755. Project: https://srinathm1359.github.io/random-latent-exploration
2. **Algorithm.** Not a novelty bonus. Sample a latent z ~ P_z (unit sphere S^{d-1} for Atari, d = 8; isotropic Gaussian for IsaacGym, d = 32). Reward r_t = R(s,a) + lambda * F(s_{t+1}, z) with F(s, z) = phi(s) . z, where phi = the value network's CNN trunk (EMA copy, tau = 0.005) followed by a frozen random linear layer; features are running-mean/std normalised. Policy and value are conditioned on z. lambda = 0.01. Each z induces a different random "goal direction", giving deep, temporally-coherent exploration a la randomised value functions, with the cost of a bonus method.
3. **Episodic assumptions. Mild.** z is resampled "at the start of each trajectory" and also on a fixed-step schedule (every 1,280 steps with 128 workers in Atari; every 16 steps in IsaacGym). In a continuing env simply resample on the step schedule (and optionally on respawn). There is no memory to reset. Value bootstrapping across z changes is handled as in the paper (z is part of the state).
4. **Observations.** Atari pixels (57 games), IsaacGym states.
5. **Robustness evidence.** The only method in this review with a *statistically reliable* all-games result: probability-of-improvement lower CI > 0.5 against PPO, NoisyNet and RND on all 57 Atari games, while "both NoisyNet and RND do not consistently improve over PPO" (POI over PPO below 0.5). Not tested on Montezuma-class hard exploration specifically as a headline; not tested in continuing settings.
6. **Code.** Official: https://github.com/Improbable-AI/random-latent-exploration (PyTorch, built on CleanRL and IsaacGymEnvs; `atari/ppo_rle.py`, `isaacgym/ppo_rle.py`).
7. **Fit: good.** Cheap, CleanRL-based, no episodic memory. Its weakness is that it is not a *coverage* signal; it should be evaluated as an alternative to, not a replacement for, RND.

### 1.9 Pseudo-counts (CTS, PixelCNN, CFN)

1. **Unifying Count-Based Exploration and Intrinsic Motivation.** Bellemare, Srinivasan, Ostrovski, Schaul, Saxton, Munos. NeurIPS 2016. arXiv:1606.01868. **Count-Based Exploration with Neural Density Models.** Ostrovski, Bellemare, van den Oord, Munos. ICML 2017. arXiv:1703.01310. **Flipping Coins to Estimate Pseudocounts for Exploration in RL (CFN).** Lobel, Bagaria, Konidaris. ICML 2023. arXiv:2306.03186.
2. **Algorithm.** Pseudo-count N_hat(s) = rho(s)(1 - rho'(s)) / (rho'(s) - rho(s)) from a sequential density model rho (CTS over 42x42 downsampled frames; PixelCNN later) with bonus beta / sqrt(N_hat). CFN replaces the density model with a regression network trained to predict Rademacher (coin-flip) labels; the squared norm of its output approximates 1/n, giving a count-like bonus without a density model.
3. **Episodic assumptions.** None (lifetime counts). Directly usable in a continuing env with an RND-style value head.
4. **Observations.** Atari pixels (all three); CFN also visual gridworld and continuous control.
5. **Robustness evidence.** Taïga 2020: CTS and PixelCNN ~5,000 on Montezuma at 200M frames (same as RND), but "pseudo-counts with a CTS model appears to struggle when the environment is an easy exploration problem"; no benefit from 1B frames. CFN: outperformed RND and density-model baselines on most of 9 tasks incl. Montezuma (Rainbow base). RLeXplore's "PseudoCounts" module (a kNN-in-embedding-space approximation, as in NGU) "benefit[s] significantly from RMS normalisation".
6. **Code.** CFN official: https://github.com/samlobel/CFN (Dopamine/Rainbow base, gin configs; Atari, visual gridworld, Ant-UMaze). I did not locate an official CTS/PixelCNN release; Dopamine does not ship pseudo-counts. RLeXplore has a PseudoCounts module.
7. **Fit: good in principle (CFN), but evidence base and code are value-based (Rainbow); porting the bonus to PPO is straightforward.**

### 1.10 2024-2026 bonus methods (brief)

* **DRND - Distributional RND.** Yang, Tao, Lyu, Li. ICML 2024. arXiv:2401.09750. Multiple random targets; bonus mixes prediction error with a pseudo-count-like variance term, addressing RND "bonus inconsistency". Online Atari + offline D4RL anti-exploration. Code: https://github.com/yk7333/DRND (MIT; `online/train.py` for Atari). No episodic assumptions. Fit: good, drop-in RND upgrade with modest evidence.
* **RDD - Random Distribution Distillation.** Fang, Yang, Tao, Lyu, Li, Shen, Li. arXiv:2505.11044 (May 2025; venue not verified). Target outputs sampled from a normal distribution so that the bonus is bounded by a pseudo-count term plus a predictor-convergence term, unifying count- and error-based bonuses. Fit: good in principle; code not verified.
* **LPM - Learning Progress Monitoring.** Hou, An, Du. ICLR 2026. arXiv:2509.25438. Rewards *improvement* in dynamics-model error (an error model predicts the previous iteration's error; bonus = decrease), provably zero on unlearnable (noisy-TV) transitions. Tested on MNIST noise, a 3D maze with 160x120 RGB, Atari. Code: https://github.com/Akuna23Matata/LPM_exploration. No episodic assumption. Fit: good candidate if stochastic distractors dominate; evidence young.
* **SND - Self-supervised Network Distillation.** Pecháč, Chovanec, Farkaš. arXiv:2302.11563 (2023, rev. 2024). RND with a target trained by a self-supervised objective; 10 hard-exploration envs. Code not verified.
* **RRP - Random Reward Perturbation.** Ma, Fu, Luo, Wu, Leong. arXiv:2506.08737 (June 2025). Zero-mean reward noise for exploration; complementary to action noise; PPO/SAC. Not pixel-validated as far as I could see.
* **RFIG - Random Feature Information Gain.** Radji, Maillard. arXiv:2607.17981 (July 2026). Random Fourier features approximate information gain, Bayesian-kernel grounding; "competitive" on control/navigation. Code not verified.
* **CAE / CAE+ - Critic as Explorer.** Li. arXiv:2503.18980 (2025, rev. 2026). Bandit-style exploration from the value network, ~10 lines of code; MuJoCo, MiniHack, Habitat; claims to beat E3B/RND/ICM/RIDE/NovelD on MiniHack. Code not verified.
* **ETD - Episodic Novelty Through Temporal Distance.** Jiang et al. ICLR 2025. arXiv:2501.15418. Contrastive temporal distance as the episodic similarity metric; CMDP results. Episodic by design.
* **DEIR.** Wan, Tang, Tian, Kaneko. IJCAI 2023. arXiv:2304.10770. Discriminative-model episodic bonus; MiniGrid/ProcGen. Code: https://github.com/swan-utokyo/deir. Episodic by design.
* **HIRE - Hybrid Intrinsic Reward.** Yuan, Li, Jin, Zeng. arXiv:2501.12627 (Jan 2025). Fusion strategies for combining several bonuses; builds on the RLeXplore finding that mixtures (E3B+RIDE, RND+others) beat single bonuses.
* **ELEMENT.** Li, Yang, Liang, Yu, Principe. arXiv:2412.03800. Lifelong kNN-entropy term + episodic average-state-entropy term, reward-free; explicitly "episodic and lifelong" multiscale design. Pixel validation not confirmed from the abstract.
* **Temporal Representations for Exploration.** Mohamed, Ji, Eysenbach, Berseth. arXiv:2603.02008 (Mar 2026). Temporal-contrastive representations prioritising states with unpredictable futures; locomotion/manipulation/embodied AI. Code not verified.
* **SIERL - Search-Inspired Exploration.** Sotirchos, Ajanović, Kober. arXiv:2602.00460 (Jan 2026). Frontier sub-goals by cost-to-come/cost-to-go. Goal-conditioned; not a bonus.
* **RePPO / ReMax.** Nishimori et al. arXiv:2606.00151 (May 2026). Exploration emerging from an expected-max-over-M-samples objective, no bonus; MinAtar, Craftax. Code: github nissymori/remax-rl (as stated in the abstract; not fetched).
* **EIPO (context).** Chen, Hong, Pathak, Agrawal. NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2211.07627. Constrained optimisation that automatically scales the intrinsic weight so the intrinsic-augmented policy never underperforms the extrinsic-only one; "matches the best baseline" across 61 Atari games. Code: https://github.com/Improbable-AI/eipo. Directly addresses the Taïga/Craftax failure mode (bonus hurts when extrinsic reward is dense).

## Part 2. Robustness and evaluation studies

### 2.1 Taïga, Fedus, Machado, Courville, Bellemare - On Bonus-Based Exploration Methods in the ALE (ICLR 2020; arXiv:2109.11052; workshop version arXiv:1908.02388)

Rainbow + {CTS pseudo-counts, PixelCNN pseudo-counts, RND, ICM, NoisyNets} vs epsilon-greedy, 200M and 1B frames, Montezuma + 7 hard-exploration games (Montezuma, Gravitar, Private Eye, Venture, Solaris, Pitfall!, Freeway) + full Atari suite. Findings: all bonus methods were tuned on Montezuma; RND/CTS/PixelCNN reach ~5,000 there, ICM does not clearly beat baselines, NoisyNets ~2,435. On easy-exploration games "RND and ICM consistently match epsilon-greedy but never beat it"; CTS struggles. Retuning on easy games yields "only limited value except for a few games". No method benefits from 1B frames on the hard set. Conclusion: Montezuma gains may be due to architecture change rather than exploration; progress "obstructed by exceptional results on a single domain".

### 2.2 Burda, Edwards, Pathak, Storkey, Darrell, Efros - Large-Scale Study of Curiosity-Driven Learning (ICLR 2019; arXiv:1808.04355)

Pure curiosity (no extrinsic reward) across 54 environments with PPO (128 envs). Feature spaces: raw pixels (does not work well anywhere), random features (strong baseline), VAE (same or worse), inverse-dynamics features (better in 55% of Atari games, generalise better e.g. Mario level transfer). Demonstrated the noisy-TV failure of prediction-error bonuses. Two methodological choices that are relevant here: (a) they treat the curiosity problem as infinite-horizon ("death is not the end"), which the RND paper later turned into the non-episodic intrinsic head; (b) advantage/reward normalisation was essential for stability across games. Code at https://pathak22.github.io/large-scale-curiosity/.

### 2.3 Andres, Villar-Rodriguez, Del Ser - An Evaluation Study of Intrinsic Motivation Techniques applied to RL over Hard Exploration Environments (CD-MAKE 2022; arXiv:2205.11184)

PPO + {COUNTS, RND, ICM, RIDE} on procedurally generated, partially observed MiniGrid tasks (MN7S8, O2Dlh, KS3R3, ...), 3 seeds. RQ1 intrinsic coefficient: static beta is not the most sample-efficient; adaptive decay (sliding-window) best, parameter decay competent but task-dependent; the NGU-style family-of-coefficients was worse except for RND (RND's bonus decays slowest). RQ2: episodic counts vs first-visit scaling - both help a lot, no clear winner; RIDE with episodic counts solved every task, RND could not solve MN7S8/O2Dlh under any weighting. RQ3: shrinking the intrinsic-module network degrades performance; with small IM nets a shared two-headed actor-critic is preferable. Note: symbolic, procedurally generated, episodic - weak transfer to a singleton pixel world, but the weighting-schedule findings are general.

### 2.4 Henaff, Jiang, Raileanu - A Study of Global and Episodic Bonuses for Exploration in Contextual MDPs (ICML 2023; arXiv:2306.03236; PMLR 202)

Taxonomy: global bonuses (RND, ICM, pseudo-counts, NovelD's RND term, AGAC's adversarial term) vs episodic bonuses (episodic counts in RIDE/NovelD, NGU's kNN, E3B). Controlled experiment on MiniHack MultiRoom-N6-Lava with |C| contexts: "the global bonus consistently obtains near-perfect performance for the singleton MDP setting where |C| = 1" but drops sharply as |C| grows, while the episodic bonus is flat. Corridors (singleton) is a counter-example where the episodic bonus alone fails and the global bonus succeeds. Framework: episodic bonuses help when the value function V_{phi,c}(z) over bonus-feature space varies a lot across contexts; global bonuses help when it varies little (singleton = zero variance). Combination: additive E3B + global gives no gain; *multiplicative* E3B x RND or E3B x NovelD gives large, significant gains on 16 MiniHack tasks (new SOTA). Pixel checks: Habitat HM3D (|C| = 1000) - episodic best, combined better than global; Montezuma (|C| = 1) - RND global works, E3B episodic fails, combined nearly matches RND. Stated limitation: the product is more robust in aggregate but "does not always match the best-performing bonus on individual tasks". Code in the E3B repo.

### 2.5 URLB - Laskin et al., Unsupervised RL Benchmark (NeurIPS 2021 D&B; arXiv:2110.15191)

12 DMC tasks, pretraining then finetuning, states and pixels; 8 methods: knowledge-based (ICM, Disagreement, RND), data-based (APT, ProtoRL), competence-based (DIAYN, APS, SMM). Findings: "none ... solve the benchmark"; no single leader - APT/ProtoRL on states, **ICM on pixels**; most algorithms lose 20-50% from states to pixels, especially Quadruped/Jaco; representation learning matters. Follow-up (Rajeswar et al., ICML 2023, "Mastering URLB from Pixels", arXiv:2209.12016) closes the gap with a Dreamer-based agent (model-based; outside this stream).

### 2.6 Crafter and Craftax

* **Crafter** (Hafner 2021, "Benchmarking the Spectrum of Agent Capabilities", pixels): unsupervised track Plan2Explore 2.1 +/- 0.1%, RND 2.0 +/- 0.1%, random 1.6%; DreamerV2 with rewards 10.0%; humans 50.5%. RND barely beats random without extrinsic reward.
* **Craftax** (Matthews et al., ICML 2024 spotlight, arXiv:2402.16801; JAX; *symbolic* observations for all baselines, episodic with max length 100,000): Craftax-1B - PPO and PPO-RNN best; ICM, RND below PPO; "none of the tested exploration methods improved performance and E3B in fact significantly reduced the reward" (approx. PPO 40-45%, PPO-RNN 45-50%, ICM 35-40%, RND 35-40%, E3B 25-30% of max reward, read off Fig. 3). E3B was nonetheless best on the hardest achievement (EAT_PLANT), "indicating that while the intrinsic reward may not be helping with overall return, it does incentivise the agent to explore different parts of the environment". Authors' hypothesis: the achievement reward is dense enough that bonuses act as distractors. Craftax-1M: all similar (~5-10%); the Simulus/M3 paper (arXiv:2502.11537) reports PPO-RNN 5.44%, RND 2.11%, E3B 1.91% on Craftax-1M **[numbers from a search snippet; not verified against the table]**. Tuned bonus hyperparameters (Tables 8-10): ICM coef 0.001, E3B coef 1.0 with ridge 0.1, RND output dim 256, RND lr 3e-4, RND loss coef 0.01, RND GAE coef 0.01. Note the Craftax RND omits observation/reward normalisation.
* 2025: Lidayan et al. (arXiv:2503.23631) compare human and agent exploration in Crafter; entropy and empowerment (not prediction-error novelty) correlate with human exploration progress.

### 2.7 NetHack Learning Environment (Küttler et al., NeurIPS 2020; arXiv:2006.13760)

IMPALA/torchbeast + RND on symbolic glyph/blstats/message inputs, following Burda's recipe (two-headed value, gamma_E 0.999 / gamma_I 0.99, non-episodic intrinsic reward, running-std normalisation; deviations: no observation normalisation, intrinsic reward divided by 10 and tanh-clipped, forward loss scaled by 0.01 "to slow down the rate at which the model becomes familiar with a given state, since the intrinsic reward often collapsed quickly despite the reward normalization"). Results: `staircase` +13.58/+6.52/+16.34 pp (monk/tourist/valkyrie), -12.96 pp (wizard); consistent gains on `pet`, `gold` (dungeon level 4.2 -> 5.0), `score` (748 -> 780 monk); consistently *worse* on `eat`; intrinsic-only agents "made no significant progress through the dungeon, even on a single fixed seed"; RND "is not sufficient for agents to learn to find the Oracle". MiniHack (Samvelyan et al. 2021) is the standard bonus testbed where E3B > NovelD/RIDE/RND/ICM (E3B paper).

### 2.8 RLeXplore (Yuan et al., TMLR 04/2025; arXiv:2405.19548; code https://github.com/RLE-Foundation/RLeXplore, MIT, PyTorch, plugs into CleanRL / SB3 / RLLTE)

Eight bonuses (ICM, RND, Disagreement, NGU, PseudoCounts, RIDE, RE3, E3B) under one PPO. Findings: (Q1) RMS observation normalisation > min-max; "RND achieving zero rewards in SuperMarioBros if observations are not normalized with RMS"; RND, NGU, PseudoCounts, RIDE all benefit. (Q2) "almost all intrinsic rewards critically require some form of reward normalization"; which normaliser is best is environment-dependent (min-max on Mario, RMS on MiniGrid). (Q3) update proportion for the auxiliary networks (1/10/50/100%): "no clear configuration that seems generally better" - must be tuned. (Q4) orthogonal init helps most methods. (Q5) LSTM memory generally *hurts* bonuses designed for global exploration. (Q6) separate intrinsic/extrinsic value heads ("TwoHead") beat naive summation on Procgen-Maze. (Q7) mixtures: E3B+RIDE best; RND or ICM mixed with others often beat any single bonus - "different intrinsic rewards can provide orthogonal gains". Their implementations reproduce or exceed the original papers (RIDE Mario 100% vs 23%).

### 2.9 EIPO (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2022) and the "bonus hurts" problem

"An overly curious agent performs worse than an agent trained with only task reward" when task reward suffices; EIPO's constrained extrinsic-intrinsic optimisation matches the best of {PPO, PPO+RND} on 61 Atari games. This, with Taïga 2020 and Craftax, is the strongest cross-study pattern: *the intrinsic weight needs to adapt, and a fixed beta chosen on one game does not transfer*.

### 2.10 Reproducibility reports

I found no ML Reproducibility Challenge / ReScience report dedicated to RND, NGU, RIDE or NovelD. The closest are: CleanRL's documented single-seed RND reproduction (7,100 vs 8,152); RLeXplore's systematic re-implementations (often exceeding paper numbers); the NLE appendix (RND bonus "often collapsed quickly despite the reward normalization" on symbolic inputs); Craftax (bonuses fail to help). NGU/Agent57 have no official code, and community ports are only validated on Pong/Breakout.

### 2.11 What is actually known about robustness (synthesis)

1. **Normalisation is not optional.** Observation whitening for random-network methods and running-std (or min-max) reward normalisation for all bonuses are the difference between working and zero reward (RLeXplore; RND paper Sec. 2.4; NLE appendix).
2. **Separate value heads for intrinsic and extrinsic returns** help (RND Sec. 2.3 and Fig. 4; RLeXplore Q6; Agent57 Sec. 3). They are required if the two streams differ in episodicity or discount.
3. **Global vs episodic depends on shared structure.** Singleton MDP -> global bonus (RND) is near-perfect where the episodic bonus can fail; many contexts -> episodic; multiplicative combination is the most robust default (Henaff 2023). For a fixed open world the prior strongly favours a global bonus plus a within-window episodic term.
4. **Bonuses rarely help when extrinsic reward is dense**, and can hurt (Taïga, Craftax, EIPO). The fix is adaptive weighting (EIPO, decay schedules per Andres 2022), not a different bonus.
5. **Bonus choice matters less than people assume.** On Montezuma RND ~ CTS ~ PixelCNN at matched budgets (Taïga); RND and NoisyNet are not reliably better than PPO across Atari (RLE paper); ICM leads pixel URLB. The method with the most reliable *all-games* improvement in a recent controlled comparison is RLE, which is not a novelty bonus at all.
6. **Stochasticity (noisy TV) remains the main known failure of prediction-error bonuses**; disagreement, LPM, and (partly) count/pseudo-count methods are the principled mitigations.
7. **Memory interacts with bonuses.** Recurrent policies hurt global-bonus methods in RLeXplore; the RND paper found RNN policies better on Montezuma at scale but less stable; a 2026 study (Malegaonkar et al., arXiv:2608.05111) finds bonuses amplify or equalise memory-architecture differences depending on whether reward supervises memory content.

## Part 3. Exploration without resets

### 3.1 Reset-free / autonomous RL (robotics lineage)

* **EARL** - Sharma, Xu, Sardana, Gupta, Hausman, Levine, Finn, "Autonomous Reinforcement Learning: Formalism and Benchmarking", ICLR 2022 (arXiv:2112.09605; code https://architsharma97.github.io/earl_benchmark/). Formalises *deployed policy evaluation* D(A) = cumulative suboptimality of the policy evaluated from the initial-state distribution during non-episodic training, and *continuing policy evaluation* C(A) = average reward over the agent's lifetime. Six **state-based** manipulation/locomotion tasks (Tabletop-Organization, Sawyer-Door, Sawyer-Peg, Franka-Kitchen, DHand-LightBulb, Minitaur-Pen), interventions every 100k-200k steps, sparse rewards plus a few demonstrations. Baselines: FBRL (forward-backward), R3L (perturbation controller with novelty seeking), VaPRL (value-accelerated curriculum), naive SAC, oracle episodic SAC. Findings: SAC "degrades drastically as the training horizon is increased"; agents drift away from the initial/goal distribution, turning the problem into a hard exploration problem; autonomously trained policies are more robust to initial-state shift. No pixels, no open world.
* **VaPRL** - Sharma, Gupta, Levine, Hausman, Finn, "Autonomous RL via Subgoal Curricula", NeurIPS 2021 (goal-conditioned curriculum from demonstrations).
* **MEDAL** - Sharma, Ahmad, Finn, "A State-Distribution Matching Approach to Non-Episodic RL", ICML 2022 (arXiv:2205.05212). Backward policy matches the demonstration state distribution via a classifier; ~40% gain on the hardest EARL task. Requires demonstrations.
* **Leave No Trace** - Eysenbach, Gu, Ibarz, Levine, ICLR 2018 (learn a reset policy jointly; abort when reset value is low) **[cited from memory; not re-fetched]**.
* **LiSP** - Lu, Grover, Abbeel, Mordatch, "Reset-Free Lifelong Learning with Skill-Space Planning", ICLR 2021 (arXiv:2012.03548). Non-episodic, non-stationary, with sink states; intrinsic skill discovery + skill-space dynamics model + planning; gridworld/MuJoCo states.
* **RISC** - "Intelligent Switching for Reset-Free RL", ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2405.01684): forward/backward agents with confidence-based switching; SOTA on reset-free benchmarks (EARL-style).
* **MoReFree** - Yang et al., "Reset-free RL with World Models", TMLR 02/2025 (arXiv:2408.09807): model-based; adapts exploration and policy learning to prioritise task-relevant states; beats MEDAL/VaPRL-type baselines without reward or demos.
* **REVERSAL-BENCH** - Shaik, Venkataraman, arXiv:2609.17745 (Sept 2026): a reversibility parameter rho across eight manipulation settings in five engines; documents the "reset-free RL cliff" - as reversibility falls, reset-free agents get "absorbed into irrecoverable states" while episodic agents keep improving; recoverability is predictable.
* **Continuing SAC** - Farrahi, Mahmood, "Learning Without Time-Based Embodiment Resets in SAC", CoLLAs 2025 (arXiv:2512.06252): removing resets causes poor state-space exploration; raising policy entropy when performance plateaus recovers it; real-robot vision task.
* **Reset-free real-world driving** - arXiv:2604.07672 (2026), 1/10-scale car learning continuously without manual resets.

Relevance: this literature is about *returning to task-relevant states without a reset mechanic* in low-dimensional robotics. Rain World gives a free "reset" on death (respawn), so the forward/backward-policy machinery is unnecessary; what transfers is (a) the C(A) continuing evaluation protocol, (b) the observation that non-episodic training turns into a hard exploration problem because of distribution drift, and (c) the REVERSAL-BENCH warning about absorbing states (e.g. getting permanently stuck in a region).

### 3.2 Lifelong / non-stationary intrinsic motivation without resets

* **Jelly Bean World** - Platanios, Saparov, Mitchell, ICLR 2020 (code https://github.com/eaplatanios/jelly-bean-world). "Non-episodic, disallowing agents from resetting the environment and retrying"; procedurally generated infinite 2D grid with items; observations are an egocentric *vision* patch (small RGB image) and a scent vector; multi-task/multi-agent/curriculum support. The only published non-episodic benchmark with image observations I found that is explicitly designed for never-ending learning.
* **Reactive Exploration** - Steinparz, Schmied, Paischer, Dinu, Patil, Bitto-Nemling, Eghbal-zadeh, Hochreiter, CoLLAs 2022 (arXiv:2207.05742; code https://github.com/ml-jku/reactive-exploration). Lifelong RL in JBW for 2M steps *without resets*, with domain shifts (reward inversion, colour swap) every 0.5-1M steps. ICM-style forward/inverse models plus a reward-prediction model detect change points and re-inflate exploration; PPO profits most, DQN fails under non-stationarity; recurrent PPO also tested. Closest existing study to "intrinsic motivation in a continuing pixel-ish world", albeit with tiny observations.
* **Continuing deep RL study** - Wan, Korenkevych, Zhu, "An Empirical Study of Deep RL in Continuing Tasks", arXiv:2501.06937 (Jan 2025; code https://github.com/facebookresearch/DeepRL-continuing-tasks). Atari and MuJoCo made continuing (time-based resets removed; Atari resets with a -1 penalty only on losing all lives; MuJoCo falls reset with a cost). DDPG/TD3/SAC/PPO/DQN. Findings: gamma 0.999 vs 0.99 "significantly hurt performance across all algorithms", reward offsets cause catastrophic failure; TD-based reward centering (Naik et al. 2024) fixes offset sensitivity and helps or maintains all algorithms; predefined resets substantially help, agent-controlled resets are hard. **Intrinsic rewards not studied.** Important for this project because RND's gamma_E = 0.999 and non-stationary intrinsic reward scale are exactly the conditions under which this study sees failures - reward centering for the extrinsic head and the running-std normalisation of the intrinsic head are the mitigations.
* **Forager** - Tang, Xiong, Hakhverdyan, Patterson, Adkins, He, Elelimy, Panahi, White, White, arXiv:2605.01131 (May 2026): lightweight *continuing* partially-observable grid world for continual RL (plasticity, memory), "never-ending learning, acting and exploration". Grid-based, not pixels.
* **COOM** (Tomilin et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B): continual RL from pixels in Doom, but a *task sequence* with episodes, not a no-reset world.
* **Persistent MDPs / RbExplore** - Ugadiarov, Skrynnik, Panov, MICAI 2021 (arXiv:2109.10173): "persistent MDP" means the simulator *can roll back* to visited states (Prince of Persia, pixels, beats ICM/RND). This is Go-Explore-style state restore and therefore **excluded** by the constraints, but note the terminology clash: "persistent" there means the opposite of "no resets".
* **Remember to be Curious** - Goli, Kerr, Reda, Jacobson, Tagliasacchi, Kanazawa, arXiv:2605.22814 (May 2026): curiosity-only agent in persistent 3D worlds (HM3D/Gibson) with an online 3D reconstruction as persistent world model plus a sequence model over RGB for episodic trajectory history; beats RL active-mapping baselines. Shows the "persistent global memory + episodic context" split re-emerging in 2026 for pixel 3D exploration (though 3D reconstruction is domain knowledge).
* **ELEMENT** (arXiv:2412.03800) and **NGU** are the two methods that explicitly name "lifelong" and "episodic" timescales; neither evaluates a no-reset setting.

### 3.3 Average-reward / continuing-task theory and intrinsic rewards

I could not find any paper that formulates an exploration bonus in the *average-reward* (differential) setting with deep function approximation and evaluates it on pixels. The relevant pieces are: reward centering (Naik, Wan, Tomar, Sutton 2024) and the Wan et al. 2025 study for the discounted-but-continuing case; the RND paper's own observation that "the extrinsic reward function is stationary whereas the intrinsic reward function is non-stationary", which is the justification for separate value heads; and RLeXplore's finding that reward normalisation is the dominant stabiliser. In practice the field handles intrinsic non-stationarity with (i) running-std normalisation of the intrinsic return, (ii) separate heads, (iii) a low intrinsic gamma (0.99) so the intrinsic critic tracks a short horizon, (iv) a predictor learning rate / update proportion chosen so the bonus decays slowly (RND, NLE), and (v) decaying or adaptive intrinsic coefficients (Andres 2022, EIPO).

### 3.4 Detachment and derailment outside Go-Explore

The terms come from Ecoffet et al. (Go-Explore, arXiv:1901.10995; "First return, then explore", Nature 2021): detachment = the bonus has been consumed around a frontier and, with catastrophic forgetting, the agent loses the ability to return to it; derailment = exploratory noise prevents reliably returning to a promising state. Outside Go-Explore, the phenomena are discussed but rarely measured: NovelD/BeBold motivate their "difference of RND" bonus by short-sightedness and detachment of count-based methods; the RND paper notes that transient bonuses must be exploited as "stepping-stones" before they disappear and ties the predictor batch size to the policy's ability to adapt (Sec. 3.4); Taïga et al. show no benefit from 1B frames, consistent with detachment-like saturation; MADE (arXiv:2106.10268) and an information-theoretic survey (arXiv:2209.08890) list "detachment, derailment and catastrophic forgetting" as issues with intrinsic rewards. In a continuing singleton world detachment is the central risk for a pure global bonus: once RND's error is low around the spawn region and the policy has forgotten the route to a far frontier, nothing pulls it back. The two model-free mitigations with evidence are (a) an episodic/windowed term that keeps rewarding within-window diversity (E3B x RND), and (b) a non-episodic intrinsic value head with a moderately long horizon so the value of distant frontiers persists in V_I (RND).

### 3.5 Does a standard benchmark for continuing, pixel-based, no-reset exploration exist?

**No.** As of this review there is no accepted benchmark that is simultaneously (i) continuing with no resets, (ii) pixel-observed at Atari-or-larger resolution, and (iii) aimed at exploration/intrinsic motivation. The nearest neighbours are Jelly Bean World (non-episodic, tiny vision patch, used once for lifelong IM), EARL (no resets, states, robotics, demos), the Wan et al. 2025 continuing Atari/MuJoCo testbeds (continuing, pixels for Atari, but about discounting/centering and still resetting on game over), Forager (continuing, grid), and Craftax/NetHack/MiniHack/Habitat (pixels or symbols, but episodic). A Rain World continuing-exploration benchmark would fill a real gap; the EARL continuing-policy-evaluation metric C(A) (lifetime average extrinsic reward) plus coverage metrics (rooms/regions visited over the lifetime, as RND and E3B/Habitat report) are the natural evaluation protocol.

## Direct answers

### (a) For a fixed singleton open world observed from pixels, with no resets, which bonus has the best evidence?

**RND, in the Burda 2018 configuration.** Reasons: it is the only bonus whose intrinsic return was designed and shown to work non-episodically (Fig. 3/4 of the paper); Henaff 2023 gives both theory (singleton MDP = zero value-variance across contexts = global bonus regime) and a pixel experiment (Montezuma: RND >> E3B); it has the best pixel-scale evidence (Atari, Habitat RGB) and the best reference code. The caveats are equally well-evidenced: on easy-exploration or dense-reward tasks it matches but does not beat epsilon-greedy/PPO (Taïga, Craftax, RLE paper), and it is fragile to normalisation and update-rate settings. The second-best-evidenced choice for the same setting is the multiplicative **E3B x RND** combination, which on Montezuma nearly matched RND and is more robust across regimes; it requires a pseudo-episode definition. Pseudo-counts (CFN) have comparable Montezuma evidence but value-based code only.

### (b) RND implementation details that matter in a continuing setting

1. **Two value heads, with the intrinsic head non-episodic.** Never zero the bootstrap for V_I at death/respawn (`int_nextnonterminal = 1`). Decide deliberately whether the extrinsic head treats death as terminal; the RND paper's "find reward, die, repeat" exploit is exactly what a continuing env permits if death is a free teleport to a rewarding region.
2. **Discounts.** gamma_I = 0.99, gamma_E = 0.999 in the paper; raising gamma_I to 0.999 hurt. Wan et al. 2025 show gamma = 0.999 is dangerous in continuing tasks with reward offsets; consider reward centering or a lower gamma_E, and keep gamma_I at 0.99.
3. **Intrinsic reward normalisation** by the running std of the discounted intrinsic return (RewardForwardFilter with gamma_I), *not* per-step reward std; no clipping of intrinsic rewards. In a lifetime with no resets the running statistics are never re-initialised - that is fine, but the filter's return estimate should also never be reset.
4. **Observation normalisation for predictor/target only**: per-pixel running mean/std, clip [-5,5], warm-started with a random-policy rollout (CleanRL: 50 x 128 steps x num_envs). Policy sees raw frames. RND on the single latest frame, not the stack.
5. **Update proportion / predictor learning rate controls how fast the bonus decays.** Burda fixes the predictor's effective batch to that of 32 envs (keep prob 0.25 at 128 envs). NLE further scaled the forward loss by 0.01 because the bonus "collapsed quickly". RLeXplore found no universally good update proportion. In a continuing env the bonus must decay slowly enough that the policy can exploit it as a stepping-stone; tune this first.
6. **Coefficients.** int_coef 1, ext_coef 2 with advantages (not rewards) combined; entropy 0.001; lr 1e-4; clip 0.1; 128 envs x 128 steps. Expect to need an adaptive intrinsic weight (EIPO-style or decay) once extrinsic reward becomes dense.
7. **Network.** Orthogonal init gain sqrt(2) (RND, RLeXplore); predictor has extra layers relative to target. CNN vs RNN policy: RNN was better at scale but less stable in the RND paper; RLeXplore found memory hurts global bonuses in general.
8. **Stochasticity.** Sticky actions already produce mild noisy-TV behaviour at room boundaries in Montezuma. Rain World's weather/creature randomness may be worse; keep Disagreement or LPM as fallback predictors.
9. **Parallel envs.** More envs helped RND monotonically (32 -> 1024) *as long as* the predictor batch was held fixed. Rain World's frame rate will cap this; the 32-env run still reached 7,570 after 1.6B frames.

### (c) Which 2-3 methods to implement first

1. **PPO + RND (CleanRL `ppo_rnd_envpool.py` recipe, PyTorch; or port the recipe faithfully to JAX - the Craftax_Baselines JAX RND is *not* faithful).** Baseline, with the continuing modifications in (b). Log rooms/regions visited over the lifetime, as RND and Habitat/E3B papers do.
2. **E3B as a windowed/pseudo-episodic bonus, multiplied into RND** (Henaff 2023 combined bonus; code in facebookresearch/e3b and RLeXplore). Reset the covariance on respawn or on a fixed window (e.g. 1-4k steps); try exponential forgetting as an ablation since no published result covers it. This directly targets detachment in a singleton world.
3. **RLE** (Improbable-AI/random-latent-exploration, CleanRL-based). Resample z on the fixed-step schedule instead of episode boundaries. It is the only method with a statistically reliable improvement across all Atari games and costs almost nothing; it is the right control for "does a novelty bonus actually help here, or does any structured exploration help?"

Honourable mentions to add once the above are running: Disagreement or LPM (if stochastic distractors attract RND), CFN (count-like bonus with Montezuma evidence), DRND (drop-in RND upgrade), and EIPO-style adaptive intrinsic weighting.
