# Option discovery for exploration: the Machado-lab line, and arXiv:2610.03604 ("Wayfarer")

Literature review prepared 2026-10-04 for a pixel-observation, continuing (no-reset, death = respawn-in-place), open-world Rain World environment with a 9-key MultiBinary action space. Constraints: general-purpose only (no game-specific tricks, no hand-designed abstractions, no hand-tuned action repeat), must work without resets / state restore, must work from pixels, no LLMs, prefer published methods with reference code.

## Part 1. arXiv:2610.03604 -- "Mastering Atari 2600 Games with Discovered Options" (Wayfarer)

**Citation.** Erik M. Lintunen and Marlos C. Machado. *Mastering Atari 2600 Games with Discovered Options.* arXiv:2610.03604v1 [cs.LG], submitted 2 Oct 2026. Affiliations: University of Alberta; Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute (Amii); Canada CIFAR AI Chair (Machado). Marked "Preprint"; no venue is listed on the arXiv page or in the PDF.
Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03604 , https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03604 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03604

**Code.** NOT released as of 2026-10-04. The paper says twice: "Our code will be shared upon publication" / "We will publish our code upon publication of this paper." No URL exists. A YouTube playlist of option videos is referenced in the paper (the URL in the PDF text is truncated; not verified). Baseline data come from DQN Zoo (Rainbow/IQN/DQN) and the DreamerV3 paper.

### Problem addressed
General, domain-agnostic option discovery that works online, from a single stream of high-dimensional (pixel) experience, and that simultaneously (i) improves exploration, (ii) accelerates credit assignment, and (iii) generalises to unseen parts of the environment. The authors claim that "no option-discovery method ... has been shown to be able to effectively tackle exploration, credit assignment, and generalisation at once when processing high-dimensional observations, let alone doing so online, from a single stream of experience."

### Method (what is learned, and how options are discovered and used)
Wayfarer is an instantiation of the lab's Representation-driven Option Discovery (ROD) cycle: experience -> representation -> intrinsic rewards -> options -> new experience. Three networks train jointly, off-policy, from one shared replay buffer D, one gradient step each every 4 env steps:

1. **Representation network** (shared conv encoder psi, Laplacian head phi, inverse-dynamics head chi).
   - *Agent-centric regulariser:* multi-step inverse dynamics (Efroni et al. 2022 style). Sample (x_t, a_t, x_{t+Delta}) from replay with Delta ~ Geometric(gamma_rep = 0.9), truncated at the episode boundary; predict the first action a_t from the concatenation of the two encoded observations with cross-entropy loss L_inv. Purpose: make the feature space capture only what the agent controls (ignore timers, passing traffic, etc.).
   - *Laplacian head:* the Augmented Lagrangian Laplacian Objective (ALLO, Gomez-Bowling-Machado ICLR 2024) estimated from replayed pairs (x_t, x_{t+Delta}): minimise sum_i <u_i, L u_i> (smoothness over temporally adjacent observations) subject to orthonormality constraints enforced with Lagrange multipliers beta and a quadratic barrier of weight b, with stop-gradients that make the true eigenfunctions (rather than any rotation of them) the only stable equilibrium. Two stability changes for the online setting: the barrier coefficient b is held *fixed* (not grown), and a symmetric log transform is applied to the outputs to compress extreme values from novel observations.
   - Joint objective: max_beta min_{psi,phi,chi} L_lap + w_aux * L_inv, with w_aux = 1.0. Only the shared encoder receives both gradients.
   - d = 6 eigenfunctions are learned.

2. **Skill network** (shared conv encoder + K IQN heads). For each non-constant eigenfunction u_i, i in {2..d}, two intrinsic rewards: r_i^+ = u_i(x_{t+n}) - u_i(x_t) and r_i^- = -(u_i(x_{t+n}) - u_i(x_t)). Because one-step rewards telescope, the n-step intrinsic return is just the undiscounted change of the eigenfunction over the horizon. K = 2(d-1) = 10 options. Each head learns primitive-action values for its intrinsic reward with n-step (n = 3) IQN updates from uniformly-sampled replay; each option's policy is greedy over primitive actions w.r.t. the mean of its value quantiles. Rewards are recomputed with the *current* representation, so options change as the representation changes. All K heads update every step; the encoder descends on the averaged gradient.

3. **Main network** (Rainbow components + IQN, noisy nets, dueling, PER, n = 3, double-Q target). It estimates task-reward values over the augmented set O = A u {options} (18 primitives + 10 options in Atari). Behaviour is risk-neutral argmax over the mean of N_sel = 32 quantiles, with NoisyNet noise as the local exploration mechanism (no epsilon-greedy). Values are learned in the h(z) = sign(z)(sqrt(|z|+1)-1)+eps z transformed space instead of reward clipping.
   - *Option execution:* call-and-return; the option terminates with fixed probability P_term = 0.2 at every step (expected duration 5 steps; with frameskip 4, 20 frames). There is no learned termination and no initiation set.
   - *Learning option values while options drift:* when the main network initiates an option, that replay entry stores the option; at every other step (including steps inside the option) the primitive action is stored. Both option values and primitive-action values are then trained with the same fixed n-step target, bootstrapping regardless of whether the option has terminated. The authors interpret this as "distilling, via planning, experience generated by options into a policy over primitive actions." Empirically (App. C.2) the agent prefers options right after discovering a new room and switches back to primitives as the room becomes familiar.

Hyperparameters (Appendix D, Table 1; reproduced from the PDF, column alignment partially garbled in extraction so a couple of pairings are best-effort): d = 6, K = 10; gamma = 0.99, n = 3; IQN quantiles 64/64/32, cosine embedding 64, Huber kappa = 1; minibatch 32 (main and skill) / 512 (representation and aux); Adam; main/skill IQN step size 5e-5 with Adam eps 3.125e-4; representation step sizes 3e-4 and 3e-5 (encoder/inverse-dynamics vs dual variables); ALLO dual init -2.0, barrier coefficient 0.5 (fixed); global-norm grad clip 30; NoisyNet sigma_0 = 0.1; PER alpha = 0.2, IS exponent 0.4 -> 1.0, priority offset 1e-10, proportional PER; P_term = 0.2; w_aux = 1.0; gamma_rep = 0.9; update period 4 env steps; target period 10,000 env steps; total 5e7 env steps (= 200M frames); max episode 27,000 env steps (108k frames, 30 min); replay 1e6, min 20,000 before training. A single configuration is used across all games.

### Experiments
- Domain: Atari 2600 "challenging set" of Badia et al. (Agent57): Beam Rider, Freeway, Montezuma's Revenge, Pitfall!, Pong, Private Eye, Skiing, Solaris, Surround, Venture. Pixel observations (frame stacking only; no memory beyond that), sticky actions, full 18-action set, no life information.
- Scale: 200M frames, 5 seeds/game (main); 100M frames, 3 seeds/game (ablations). Single-stream agent (one learner, one env). Evaluation = mean episodic return over the last 10% of training; human-normalised median/IQM/mean/optimality gap with rliable 95% CIs.
- Baselines: Rainbow, IQN, DQN (DQN Zoo data; note those runs used minimal action sets, no sticky actions and life info, which the authors say may favour the baselines) and DreamerV3 (parallel envs, model-based).
- Episodic vs continuing: **strictly episodic** (30-minute episode time limit; Delta sampling "truncated at the episode boundary"). The paper does not discuss continuing/non-episodic settings at all.

### Claimed results
- Aggregate at 200M frames: Wayfarer > Rainbow, IQN, DQN; roughly ties DreamerV3 (Wayfarer HNS mean 0.54, IQM 0.65, median 0.69). Under AUC it is "comparable to or better than DreamerV3 on every metric".
- Largest gains in Montezuma's Revenge and Private Eye; leads in Venture; beats Dreamer in Solaris and Rainbow in Skiing; no agent gets positive return in Pitfall!.
- Ablations at 100M frames: removing options costs 2.51x (median), 1.44x (IQM), 1.21x (mean). A DCEO-style variant (epsilon-greedy, options used only for exploration, no option values) is "competitive in the games that are hardest to explore but performs worse overall". Removing agent-centric (inverse-dynamics) regularisation hurts. NoisyNet > epsilon-greedy when option values are learned.
- Qualitative: options that cross puddles/holes in Pitfall!, an option reaching the gun in Private Eye by 6% of training, and options discovered in room 1 of Montezuma's Revenge transferring to rooms 2 and 5 without further learning.
- Appendix I: Wayfarer plateaus early in "games requiring continual exploration"; the authors attribute this to implicit step-size decay from growing weight norms under LayerNorm and show that Normalize-and-Project (NaP) lifts Montezuma's Revenge to > 7,000 mean return within 200M frames.

### Limitations stated by the authors
- No memory beyond frame stacking; plasticity loss / plateaus (motivating NaP); no planning with options.
- All agents "struggle on this benchmark"; Pitfall! unsolved.
- Learning option values while options drift is described as "one of the biggest challenges"; the fixed-n-step-regardless-of-termination target is a simplification.
- Code not yet released.

### Fit to the Rain World constraints (candid assessment)
Positives: fully online, single-stream, from pixels, no resets or state restore required by the algorithm itself, no game knowledge, no hand-tuned action repeat (options are the temporal extension; random termination is the only "repeat" hyperparameter), no subgoal/abstraction engineering. The agent-centric regulariser is well suited to Rain World's exogenous dynamics (rain timer, creatures, weather) that the agent cannot control. The ROD loop is exactly the "discover -> explore -> re-learn" cycle you asked about.
Negatives/risks: (1) no reference code; re-implementing three coupled networks with ALLO + IQN + PER faithfully is substantial work and the paper's own ablation table shows the pieces interact. (2) Everything is episodic; the Delta-sampling truncation and the 30-minute episode cap are baked in, and the main network bootstraps from experience generated in episodes with resets. Nothing in the method conceptually needs resets, but there is zero evidence in a continuing setting. (3) Atari's 18 discrete actions vs your MultiBinary(9) (512 joint actions): both the skill heads and the main head are argmax-over-discrete-actions; you would need a factored/branching head (generic, but untested in this method). (4) Scale: 200M frames, single stream; the authors already see plasticity-induced plateaus in long-horizon exploration games, which is exactly your regime. (5) d = 6 eigenfunctions is tiny for a world the size of Rain World; the smoothest eigenfunctions will encode very coarse "which region am I in" structure, which is good for far travel but not for local puzzles.

## Part 2. The Machado-lab line on option discovery for exploration

Shared vocabulary. Graph Laplacian L = D - W of the state-transition graph; its smallest-eigenvalue eigenvectors (proto-value functions, PVFs) vary slowly over the state space. Successor representation (SR) Psi^pi(s, s') = E[sum_t gamma^t 1{S_t = s'} | S_0 = s]; Machado et al. show the SR under a random walk shares eigenvectors with the normalised Laplacian, so "eigen-SR" and "eigen-Laplacian" options coincide. An *eigenpurpose* is the intrinsic reward r_e(s, s') = e(s') - e(s) for eigenvector e; an *eigenoption* is the option whose policy maximises that reward. The ROD cycle (Machado JMLR 2023 / AAAI-25) is: collect samples -> learn representation (SR/Laplacian) -> derive intrinsic rewards -> learn option policies -> define options (initiation/termination) -> use them to collect new samples -> repeat.

### 2.1 Eigenoptions -- Machado, Bellemare, Bowling, "A Laplacian Framework for Option Discovery in RL", ICML 2017
Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00956 , https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/machado17a.html
Code (verified, listed on GitHub profile and in the paper footnote): https://github.com/mcmachado/options (Python, last updated 2017).
Algorithm: (1) Build the adjacency matrix (tabular) or an incidence matrix of observed transition differences phi(s') - phi(s) (sample-based); (2) eigendecompose L (or SVD the incidence matrix; Thm 5.1 shows equivalence); (3) for each eigenvector e define eigenpurpose r_e(s,s') = e(s') - e(s); (4) solve each eigenpurpose with value iteration / Q-learning over the action set augmented with a "terminate" action; the option terminates where q_e(s, a) <= 0 for all a (Thm 3.1: termination at local extrema of e); initiation set = all states where continuing has positive value; (5) add options to the action set and run Q-learning. Exploration benefit measured by *diffusion time* (expected steps of a random walk between random state pairs).
Pixels/scale: tabular 4-room, 10x10 grid, I-Maze (exact Laplacian). Atari (Freeway, Montezuma's Revenge) only as "anecdotal evidence": representation = the 1,024-bit emulator RAM (not pixels), SVD of a 25,000-row incidence matrix, option policies obtained by greedy one-step look-ahead with the emulator; 1,024 options inspected by hand.
Episodic/continuing: tabular episodic tasks; the Laplacian itself is reset-agnostic.
Robustness: fairly robust to the number of options (best with 64 in 4-room); no deep-RL robustness evidence.

### 2.2 "Eigenoption Discovery through the Deep Successor Representation" -- Machado, Rosenbaum, Guo, Liu, Tesauro, Campbell, ICLR 2018
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11089 ; OpenReview ICLR 2018.
Code: none found in the paper or on Machado's GitHub (a 2018 third-party reimplementation exists, e.g. https://github.com/DaomingLyu/EigenOption-Critic_SR for the related Eigenoption-Critic; not official).
Algorithm: (1) Learn a latent representation phi(s) from raw pixels with a network that predicts the next frame given the action (Oh et al. 2015 reconstruction module, needed because zero is a fixed point of the SR loss); (2) learn successor features psi(s) = E[sum gamma^t phi(S_t)] by TD on a *fixed dataset of 500k transitions collected by a uniform random policy*; (3) eigendecompose/SVD the matrix of successor features; (4) eigenpurposes r_e(s,s') = e^T(phi(s') - phi(s)); (5) learn each option's policy (DQN-style) with terminate action as in 2017. Handles stochastic transitions (SR is a learned expectation) and does not need the Laplacian to be given.
Pixels/scale: tabular 4-room (diffusion time, matches PVF-based eigenoptions after enough samples) and Atari Bank Heist, Freeway, Montezuma's Revenge, Ms. Pac-Man from raw pixels; *qualitative only* (state-visitation density plots; options reach doors/ladders in Montezuma). Failed to find meaningful options in Freeway. Two-phase (collect -> discover), options not used to improve game score.
Episodic/continuing: offline data; no return-based evaluation.

### 2.3 Covering options -- Jinnai, Park, Abel, Konidaris, "Discovering Options for Exploration by Minimizing Cover Time", ICML 2019 (Brown; not Machado's lab, but the direct precursor of CEO)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00606 ; Code (verified on author's site): https://github.com/jinnaiyuu/Optimal-Options-ICML-2019
Algorithm: expected cover time of a random walk is upper-bounded by a function of the algebraic connectivity lambda_2(L). Repeatedly: compute the Fiedler vector (2nd-smallest eigenvector), add a *point option* from the state with the largest value to the state with the smallest value (and vice versa), recompute, until k options. Each option policy = shortest path between the two states. Tabular only (9x9 grid, four-room, Taxi, Towers of Hanoi, Parr's maze, Race Track); compared to (point-option) eigenoptions and betweenness options.
Deep follow-up: Jinnai, Park, **Machado**, Konidaris, "Exploration in RL with Deep Covering Options", ICLR 2020 (https://openreview.net/forum?id=SkeIyaVtwB ; PDF https://jinnaiyuu.github.io/pdf/papers/ICLR-20.pdf). Learns only the second eigenfunction f with the Wu-Tucker-Nachum (2019) spectral graph-drawing objective, sets the termination set to states with f below its k-th percentile (under-explored region), initiation = complement, trains the option policy with reward for reaching the termination set, adds options one at a time to a higher-level agent that values them with task reward. Domains: Pinball (continuous state, discrete actions), three MuJoCo Point tasks (DDPG), and *qualitative* Atari (Montezuma, Ms. Pac-Man, Amidar). Episodic. **No code repository for the ICLR 2020 version** was found (the author's software page only lists the ICML-2019 tabular code).

### 2.4 "Temporal Abstraction in RL with the Successor Representation" -- Machado, Barreto, Precup, Bowling, JMLR 24(80):1-69, 2023
Links: https://jmlr.org/papers/v24/21-1213.html , https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05740
Code: none released (pseudocode in Appendix C).
Content: the unifying paper. (1) Formalises the ROD cycle; (2) shows SR-based eigenoptions and *covering eigenoptions (CEO)*: learn the SR by TD under the current (option-augmented) random policy, take the eigenvector of the SR with the largest eigenvalue (equivalently the Laplacian's smallest non-trivial), create two options that go to its argmax/argmin, add them to the option set, re-learn the SR, repeat -- the explicit "discover -> explore -> better representation -> better options" loop; (3) uses successor *features* and the Option Keyboard / GPI to combine learned options into exponentially many new behaviours without further learning; (4) demonstrates that broad initiation sets beat point options online and that online covering options are brittle because their point initiation sets are rarely sampled.
Pixels/scale: **tabular only** (four-room; Pinball shown for illustration). Episodic navigation with 1,000-step episodes; diffusion time and reward-accumulation metrics; 50 runs, 99% CIs.
Limitations stated: how to choose the number of options is unclear; online covering options drift from closed-form ones; representation phi is assumed given.

### 2.5 Deep Covering Eigenoptions (DCEO) -- Klissarov & Machado, "Deep Laplacian-based Options for Temporally-Extended Exploration", ICML 2023
Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11181 , https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/klissarov23a.html , project page https://sites.google.com/view/dceo/home
Code (verified): https://github.com/mklissa/dceo -- JAX on Google's Dopamine ("full_rainbow_dceo.gin"), Atari/Montezuma config provided; README also contains a step-by-step guide to integrating Laplacian options into another codebase (representation loss, option training, option execution). Third-party JAX reimplementation with Craftax configs: https://github.com/bath-reinforcement-learning-lab/jaxhrl (gymnax-only; 3 stars; unverified quality).
Algorithm (fully online, single loop):
1. Learn d eigenfunctions f_1..f_d of the Laplacian with the *generalised graph drawing objective* (Wang et al. ICML 2021; a reweighted Wu et al. 2019 objective) from replayed consecutive observation pairs; an ablation shows the original Wu et al. objective fails to recover eigenfunctions, the generalised one succeeds.
2. For each eigenfunction define the intrinsic reward r_i(s,s') = f_i(s') - f_i(s); train d option policies (DQN/Rainbow heads) off-policy on these rewards from the same replay buffer.
3. Exploration: the task agent is epsilon-greedy; when exploring, with probability mu it samples an option uniformly and follows it until termination, otherwise takes a random primitive action. Termination is random with probability 1/D (D = 10). No option values are learned; options are never selected greedily.
4. Repeat forever: new experience refines the Laplacian, which refines the options (ROD cycle online). Figure 14 of the paper shows the fully online version performs like a two-phase version.
Pixels/scale: tabular gridworlds (nine-rooms, maze, 2x2 Rubik's cube; 15% action noise); MiniGrid pixel variants (Escape room, Rooms with obstacles, Obstructed Key); MiniWorld 3D first-person FourRooms and MyWayHomeSparse; Atari Montezuma's Revenge (score 2,500 "in only a few million steps", DCEO repo reports 200M frames budget). Pixel envs use a 2-layer conv encoder. Hyperparameters: d = 10 (5 in high-dim tasks), D = 10, mu in {0.2, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} tuned per env, lr 1e-4.
Baselines: DDQN, count-based bonus 1/sqrt(n(s)) with perfect counts, epsilon_z-greedy (action repeat), RND, DIAYN, CIC.
Episodic/continuing: episodic (<= 100-step gridworld episodes; standard Atari episodes) but nothing in the algorithm uses resets.
Robustness evidence: the paper's strongest selling point. In the non-stationary nine-rooms experiment (start/goal moved and hallways closed, with no signal to the agent) DCEO re-discovers eigenoptions and recovers within a few thousand steps while count-based and RND stagnate. State coverage >= baselines across topologies. Only method with consistent positive reward in 3D MiniWorld.
Stated limitations: options have no values (no credit assignment benefit), no auxiliary-task representation benefit, no planning; option selection probability mu is tuned per environment; Rubik's cube needed tabular agents.

### 2.6 Proper Laplacian Representation Learning (ALLO) -- Gomez, Bowling, Machado, ICLR 2024
Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10833 , https://openreview.net/forum?id=7gLfQT52Nn
Code (verified): https://github.com/tarod13/laplacian_dual_dynamics (JAX/Haiku; `python train_laprepr.py <label>`; ALLO default; YAML configs).
Algorithm: max over duals beta, min over u of sum_i <u_i, L u_i> + sum_{j<=k} beta_jk (<u_j, [[u_k]]> - delta_jk) + b sum (<u_j, [[u_k]]> - delta_jk)^2, with stop-gradient [[.]] and asymmetric (triangular) constraints; Lemma 2: equilibria are exactly eigenvector permutations; Theorem 1: the sorted eigenvectors are the only stable equilibrium of gradient ascent-descent when b > 2; duals recover eigenvalues (beta_jj* = -2 lambda_j). Removes the hyperparameters GGDO needed and the rotation ambiguity.
Pixels/scale: 12 gridworld mazes/rooms, both (x,y) and raw-pixel inputs, up to 1M transitions, d = 11, 256-unit MLPs; robust across environments without retuning while GGDO fails on small ones. Uniform random-walk data; not an RL agent.
Limitations: barrier-coefficient dynamics not fully understood; downstream use of eigenvalues left to future work. Note Wayfarer deviates (fixed b, log-transform) to survive non-stationary online data.

### 2.7 Count-based exploration with the SR -- Machado, Bellemare, Bowling, AAAI 2020
Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.11622 , https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/5955
Code (verified): https://github.com/mcmachado/count_based_exploration_sr (Python 3 + ALE; tabular and DQN variants; MIT; last commit 2019).
Algorithm: while the SR is being learned by TD, its L1 norm ||psi(s)||_1 grows with visitation (Theorem: the substochastic SR implicitly counts visits), so r_int = beta / ||psi(s)||_1 is a count-like bonus with no density model. Tabular: Sarsa+SR and ESSR match R-Max/E^3-class sample efficiency on RiverSwim/SixArms. Deep: DQN with mixed Monte-Carlo (MMC) returns + successor-feature head on the DQN features; bonus = inverse 1-norm of the normalised successor features. Atari Freeway, Gravitar, Montezuma's Revenge, Private Eye, Solaris, Venture at 100M frames with sticky actions; outperforms pseudo-count DQN variants and RND at that budget in 4/5 games (RND at 2B frames is still better).
Pixels: yes (Atari). Episodic: yes, but the bonus is reset-agnostic and is the simplest reset-free, pixel-based method in this line. Not an option method.

### 2.9 2024-2026 follow-ups from the lab (from Machado's publication list; verified individually where noted)
- **Kotamreddy & Machado, "A Study of Value-Aware Eigenoptions", RLC 2025 Workshop on Inductive Biases in RL**, arXiv:2507.09127 (verified). Learns option values for eigenoptions (intra-option Q-learning tabular; hierarchical Double-DQN with per-option networks, DVAEO, from 52x52x3 pixels in Four/Nine-rooms MiniGrid). Findings directly relevant here: pre-specified eigenoptions help exploration *and* credit assignment in tabular settings; **online discovery "can bias the agent's experience too strongly and hinder learning"**; under function approximation gains were minimal; value-based termination is brittle with neural nets so they fall back to fixed 10-step termination. No code found.
- **Klissarov, Bagaria, Luo, Konidaris, Precup, Machado, "Discovering Temporal Structure: An Overview of HRL", arXiv:2506.14045 (v2 Sept 2026)** -- survey; useful map of the field.
- **Chandrasekar & Machado, "The Laplacian Keyboard: Beyond the Linear Span", NeurIPS 2026, arXiv:2602.07730** -- builds a task-agnostic behaviour basis from Laplacian eigenvectors (guaranteed to contain the optimal policy for rewards in the span) and a meta-policy that composes them; zero-shot bounds plus improved sample efficiency. Environments/pixels/code not verified (abstract only). Precursor workshop paper: "Towards an Option Basis to Optimize All Rewards", RLC 2025 workshop (OpenReview blocked).
- **Tse, Chandrasekar, Machado, "Reward-Aware Proto-Representations in RL", NeurIPS 2025** and **Tse & Machado, "DROGO: Default Representation Objective via Graph Optimization", NeurIPS 2026, arXiv:2602.00403** -- the default representation (DR), a reward-aware SR alternative; DROGO learns its principal eigenvector with a neural objective (like ALLO for the DR) and uses it for reward shaping, exploration and option discovery. ## Part 3. The one candidate to run in a continuing, pixel-based open world

**Pick: DCEO (Klissarov & Machado, ICML 2023) as the code base, upgraded with the two Wayfarer components that are cheap to add (ALLO-with-fixed-barrier for the Laplacian head, and multi-step inverse-dynamics regularisation of the representation encoder). Treat Wayfarer's "learn option values in the main network" as a second-phase experiment, not the first run.**

Why this and not the others:
- It is the only method in the line with official, pixel-scale, fully-online code (`mklissa/dceo`, JAX/Dopamine) whose README explicitly explains how to port the three components into another agent. Wayfarer has no code; the JMLR/ICML-2017 methods are tabular or need the emulator; ICLR-2018 is offline and qualitative; deep covering options has no code.
- Nothing in DCEO requires resets, state restore, or episode boundaries: the Laplacian is learned from consecutive observation pairs in replay, option policies are learned off-policy from the same buffer, option termination is a memoryless coin flip. Death-as-respawn simply appears as an unusual edge in the transition graph (see risk 2).
- It is the only method with explicit robustness evidence under non-stationarity (goal/topology shifts with no signal), which is the property a one-lifetime open world needs: eigenfunctions and options keep re-fitting as the explored region grows.
- It carries no game-specific knowledge: the only exploration-relevant hyperparameters are d (number of eigenfunctions), D (expected option length) and mu (fraction of exploratory steps that use an option). epsilon_z-greedy/action repeat is one of its *baselines*, so you are not smuggling in action repeat.
- Wayfarer's ablation says the DCEO-style variant is "competitive in the games that are hardest to explore" and only loses overall because it does not learn option values. For a pure exploration study that is the right trade: Kotamreddy & Machado 2025 show that learning option values from online-discovered, drifting options can bias experience and hinder learning, and Wayfarer only solved that with a specific IQN/PER/NoisyNet stack that you would have to rebuild without code.

What would need adapting (all generic, none game-specific):
1. **Continuing setting.** Remove episode-boundary truncation when sampling (x_t, x_{t+Delta}) pairs for the Laplacian/inverse-dynamics losses (Wayfarer uses Delta ~ Geometric(0.9); DCEO uses consecutive pairs). Use discounted continuing Q-learning (or average-reward / reward-centering) for the task head; the option heads already use discounted intrinsic returns and need no change. Report coverage/novelty over wall-clock lifetime rather than per-episode return.
2. **Death/respawn edges.** The intrinsic reward f_i(x_{t+1}) - f_i(x_t) is evaluated across the respawn transition, so an option can learn to die to "teleport" to the spawn point if that moves the eigenfunction in its favoured direction, and the Laplacian smoothness term will try to pull the death state and the respawn state together. Both are generic consequences of non-local transitions, not Rain World specifics. The clean fix is to treat the death transition as the end of a trajectory in the replay buffer (no (s, s') pair across it for either loss; no bootstrapping of the intrinsic return across it). The `info` death-edge flag you already emit is exactly what is needed; this is environment-level structure (a discontinuity in the data stream), not a hand-designed abstraction. Run the ablation with and without masking.
3. **MultiBinary(9) actions.** DCEO/Wayfarer heads are argmax over a discrete action set. Options: (a) enumerate the 512 joint actions as one discrete head (works for Rainbow-style heads; the option-count K and d are unaffected); (b) a factored/branching head with 9 two-way outputs and a shared value stream (Action Branching / BDQ style) for both the skill heads and the task head. The inverse-dynamics head becomes a 9-way multi-label sigmoid cross-entropy, which is trivial. Prefer (a) first to stay closest to the reference implementation; switch to (b) only if the 512-way head is slow to learn.
4. **Encoder.** DCEO's 2-layer conv is for 7x7-cell MiniGrid frames; use the Atari/Impala encoder from the Dopamine config (the Montezuma gin file) for Rain World frames; downsample to 84x84 grayscale or 96x96 RGB with 4-frame stacks as the paper does. Keep three *separate* encoders (representation, skills, task) as both DCEO and Wayfarer do; the skills encoder is shared across K heads.
5. **Laplacian head upgrade.** Replace GGDO with ALLO using Wayfarer's online-stability choices: fixed barrier b (0.5 in Wayfarer; ALLO's theory wants b > 2, so sweep {0.5, 2, 5}), dual init -2.0, symmetric log on outputs, duals updated with a slower step size. Add the inverse-dynamics regulariser (w_aux = 1.0) because Rain World is full of exogenous motion (rain, creatures, lighting) that pure slowness would latch onto.
6. **Plasticity.** Both DCEO's non-stationarity results and Wayfarer's Appendix I say the same thing: in long continual exploration the Laplacian must keep moving. Add LayerNorm + Normalize-and-Project (NaP) or weight-norm control to all three networks from day one; the lab's own continual-RL papers (Abbas et al. 2023; Lewandowski et al. 2025) are the fallback if plateaus appear.

Key hyperparameters and starting values (from the DCEO paper/repo and Wayfarer Table 1):
- d (eigenfunctions): DCEO 10 (5 in high-dim tasks), Wayfarer 6 -> start 8-10 for a world as large as Rain World; options K = d (DCEO, one direction) or 2(d-1) (Wayfarer, both directions; recommended).
- Option duration: DCEO D = 10 (P_term = 0.1); Wayfarer P_term = 0.2. Sweep P_term in {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}; longer durations favour far travel in big maps.
- mu (probability an exploratory step launches an option): DCEO tuned per env in {0.2, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9}; start 0.8 with an epsilon schedule 1.0 -> 0.01 over ~4M frames (Dabney et al. schedule used by Wayfarer's epsilon-greedy ablations). In a continuing run, do not decay epsilon below ~0.05.
- Learning rates: 1e-4 (DCEO, all nets); Wayfarer 5e-5 (IQN heads), 3e-4 encoder / 3e-5 duals. Replay 1M, min 20k, batch 32 (Q heads) / 512 (representation), target period 10k env steps, update every 4 env steps, gamma 0.99, n-step 3.
- Laplacian pair sampling: gamma_rep = 0.9 geometric Delta (Wayfarer) vs consecutive (DCEO).
- Frame skip 4 (standard, not hand-tuned per game; keep fixed and report it).

Best code to start from: https://github.com/mklissa/dceo (official, JAX + Dopamine, full_rainbow_dceo.gin; README has the porting guide). Take the ALLO loss from https://github.com/tarod13/laplacian_dual_dynamics (official, JAX/Haiku). If you need a gymnax-speed sandbox for hyperparameter sweeps before touching Rain World, https://github.com/bath-reinforcement-learning-lab/jaxhrl has a DCEO reimplementation with Craftax configs (third-party, unverified). Use https://github.com/mcmachado/count_based_exploration_sr only as the reset-agnostic "simple bonus" comparison baseline (it is old TF/ALE code; re-implementing the 1/||psi||_1 bonus on top of your DQN is easier than running it).

Main risks, in order:
1. **Laplacian representation learning from pixels at open-world scale.** All quantitative evidence is MiniGrid/MiniWorld/one Atari game; Wayfarer is 10 Atari games with d = 6. In a world with hundreds of rooms, the d smoothest eigenfunctions will be dominated by global region structure and will keep rotating as the frontier moves; ALLO's stop-gradient fixes rotation at equilibrium, but online data is never at equilibrium (Wayfarer had to freeze b and log-compress outputs; Ahmed et al. ICML 2025 give convergence only under projected online GD). Expect to monitor the eigenvalue estimates (ALLO's duals) as a health signal and to see options re-target as the map grows. Mitigation: larger replay, slower representation step size than the Q heads, NaP.
2. **Option termination/initiation in a continuing world.** Memoryless termination (P_term) is the only reset-free scheme in this line with deep evidence; value-based termination (ICML 2017 Theorem 3.1) is brittle with neural nets (Kotamreddy & Machado 2025 fell back to fixed 10 steps). The choice of P_term is the one "temporal" hyperparameter you cannot avoid, and in a huge map short options barely move the agent while long options can walk it into death loops. Sweep it; consider making termination depend on the sign of the option's own Q (the 2017 rule) only as a later experiment.
3. **Death/respawn as a graph shortcut** (see adaptation 2) -- an option that learns to die to maximise an eigenfunction is the most likely failure mode on the first run.
4. **Online discovery biasing the data stream.** Documented by the lab itself (Kotamreddy & Machado 2025): in one lifetime the early, inaccurate options shape all later data. DCEO's random-option-on-epsilon design is the least biased variant; keep a non-trivial fraction of pure random primitive exploration (1 - mu) for exactly this reason.
5. **No reference result in a reset-free setting from anyone in this line.** AgarCL (the lab's own non-episodic benchmark) has not, as far as I could verify, been used with eigenoptions or DCEO. Your run would be the first such evidence; budget for the possibility that the method needs the plasticity fixes above before it shows anything.

Second choice, if the Laplacian stack proves too fragile: the AAAI-2020 SR-norm count bonus (one extra head, reset-agnostic, pixel-tested at 100M frames), accepting that it is a bonus method rather than a temporal-abstraction method.
